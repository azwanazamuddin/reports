Policy simulation at the converged estimate

Three rules, one for each kind of constraint, simulated at the 11 November estimate with every parameter frozen. Each rule changes one input. The same random seed runs in every scenario, and the indicators are exact model expectations from the forward sweep, so a difference against base is the rule's effect, not sampling noise.

Setup

Estimate
nfxp_params_20261111_200908, LL −34,414.815, converged (projected gradient 1.3e-6), all Hessian eigenvalues positive; robust standard errors in the estimation record. Its spec and data sidecars are replayed in every run.
Rules
Shops close 2 h later (institutional): the opening-hours table with every shop closing 120 min later. Drivers aged 75+ give up the car (means): their household car access is removed, so they use the graph without the car. Work starts 60 min later (anchor): every worker's fixed start, and with it the exact work end, moves 60 min.
Runs
Full sample, 3 repetitions for sampled paths, seed 42, 6 shards per scenario, about 1 h each on this machine. Welfare is the change in each person's V(x₀), the log-sum over their feasible days, divided by |βcost|.
Direction checks
Written before the runs: a relaxing rule never lowers V, a tightening rule never raises it, and a person the rule does not reach does not move.

Reading

About two thirds of the train drop comes from the PT service an hour later being worse for these commutes: the timetable shifted with the day keeps 86% of workers' train trips, against 61% with the real timetable. Morning and evening trips recover alike (88% and 84% kept). Closed steps are only part of it; the rest is the departure-step travel times and how arrivals fit the fixed work start. Which of those matters most has not been separated. The remaining third is the day's position on the clock, of which the evening home rates are about a fifth of the whole.

Caveat

The timetable is from 2021 to 2025; the survey is from 2012 and 2014. The shifted-timetable run gives non-workers the shifted service too, so its whole-sample shares mix two changes; the comparison uses workers only.
