1. Convergence

Newton decrement 2.256e-07 LL <= 0.05 on an exact positive definite Hessian (gradient 1.350e-05). The exact Hessian at the estimate (computed through the worker pool) has all eigenvalues positive, 9.95 to 5.28×108. After scaling to unit diagonal the smallest is 0.0092. The three weakest directions all combine neighbouring time-of-day knots: home 12:00 to 15:00, and leisure and home at 21:00 to 22:00.

The specification compared with the 26 October origin estimate:

  • all 9,077 diary files;
  • minimum stay 0 steps for shopping and leisure;
  • start constants before work for shopping and leisure;
  • canonical states, which erase duration and mode memory that no later utility reads. This makes the graph smaller and leaves the likelihood unchanged.

3. Value of travel time

The value of a travel minute by mode is (μ(a,t) − βm) / βcost, measured against the activity it displaces. The median over hours, against home, in yen per minute:

PT 15.3 bicycle 37.4 walk 49.4 car 55.0

The trip-level MNL gives car 101.9 bicycle 76.9 walk 46.9 PT 19.1.

The PT and bicycle time coefficients are positive (beta_time_pt +0.0274, t 9.5), as in every estimate since 22 October. For car 2.1%, bicycle 2.2%, walk 2.2%, PT 9.0% of activity person-minutes, the value of a travel minute is ≤ 0, that is, the model would rather ride than stay. This matters for the BRT result below.

A faster PT trip can LOWER welfare here. With beta_time_pt > 0, a saved PT minute is worth only μ(a,t) − βpt, and that is negative where μ is small (home 11:00, shopping 07:00 to 09:00). So the losers in this table follow from the sign of the PT time coefficient, not from noise.

6. Likelihood check and the canonical encoding

The base simulation scores the diary on the ordinary (non-canonical) graph: LL -42,459.14 on 6,487 persons and 376,761 steps. The estimator, on the canonical graph, scored the same persons at -42,462.12 on 376,773 steps. The 2.98 LL gap comes from the 12 extra steps.

The ordinary encoding audit (28 October) and the canonical one (30 October) cover the same diaries: 617,372 and 617,372 rows. 12 steps of 10 persons change outcome. 12 become scored under canonical states and 0 stop being scored.

Reading. All 12 are steps where the ordinary graph has no state carrying the diary's exact memory label. Nine are leisure stays that remember the arrival mode (car, car passenger, walk). Three are work steps that remember car passenger. Canonical states drop memory that no later utility reads, so the diary's state maps onto the merged node and the step is scored. Nothing is lost in the other direction. The 42 persons with an infeasible matched edge are the same in both audits. The canonical likelihood therefore scores a superset of the ordinary one, and the 12 extra steps are an encoding artefact of the ordinary graph, not new behaviour.
