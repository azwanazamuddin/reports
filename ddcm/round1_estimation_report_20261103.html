Round 1: estimation results and fit

Round 1: what changed and what it fixed

Round 1 is the first respecification after the final 30 October estimate. It changes three things and adds no parameter:

  1. No empty tours. A trip that leaves home for the home zone must reach an activity before the person is home again (constraint #15). The simulation had made about 1,160 home → travel → home loops a day that the diary never shows as a tour. The 606 observed loops on 30 October were short out-and-back stops that the old encoding merged into a single trip; round 1 keeps those stops.
  2. Short shopping and leisure stops are kept (--short-stops recover). A stop shorter than one 15-minute step used to be merged into the trip around it; now it is kept as a stop when the encoder can place it.
  3. Persons with pick-up/drop-off or hospital-visit stops are left out (--exclude-unmodelled-short). These purposes are not in the model and would need their own constraints. 6,299 persons are scored, against 6,487 on 30 October.

The likelihood is not comparable across the two runs, because the persons and the encoding of their days differ. The fit below can be compared, because each run is set against the diary as its own model sees it. Both simulations use the corrected random seeds (4 November). The tour structure is now reproduced: tours per day, days with no tour, and the starts of shopping and leisure tours from home all match. What remains is the chaining after the first stop (leisure → home is over-simulated and leisure → leisure under-simulated) and stop durations (simulated shopping stops are too long: the median is 60 min against 30 for non-workers). Neither has a parameter yet, so both are candidates for round 2.

9. Likelihood check and the canonical encoding

The base simulation scores the diary on the ordinary graph: LL -39,090.68 on 6,299 persons and 366,459 steps. The estimator scored the same persons on the canonical graph at -39,095.94 on 366,470 steps. The 5.26 LL gap is the 11 extra steps.

For round 1 only the canonical encoding audit was run (2 November, 599,759 rows), so there is no step-by-step comparison with an ordinary-graph audit as there was for 30 October. The pattern matches 30 October: the canonical graph scores 11 steps more than the ordinary graph (366,470 against 366,459), on the same 6,299 persons, and its likelihood is lower by about the cost of those steps. On 30 October the same comparison gave 12 extra steps, all of them diary states whose exact memory label (arrival mode, or duration) has no state in the ordinary graph and which the canonical graph merges into the node that carries the same future.
