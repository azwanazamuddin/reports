**July – September 2026.** The claim, as finally written and defended.

The thesis was defended on **10 August 2026** and the manuscript is dated **September 2026**. It
has seven chapters: Introduction, Background, Model Specification, A Scalable Computational
Framework, Higashihiroshima, Conclusion and Data Description. The manuscript itself is not
published here; the defense deck is the public record.

## What it claims

Three contributions, all resting on one observation: a log-sum adds up every feasible day, so
nothing may be discarded for being unlikely, only for being impossible.

1. A forward search under the space-time prism keeps only the states a person can physically reach,
   exactly and not approximately.
2. The pruned graph is acyclic, so a whole time layer solves in one call.
3. Feasibility is shared where preference is not, so one graph serves everyone facing the same
   constraints.

The full statement, with figures, is on [the PhD research page](/) under *the Master's framework*.
